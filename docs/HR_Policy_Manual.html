Section 1: Employing Staff

1.1 Recruitment Policy

Purpose

To ensure a fair, consistent and effective recruitment process that attracts and selects the best candidates for all positions within the company.

Scope

This policy applies to all recruitment activities across all departments and levels within the organization.

Principles

  • All recruitment will be conducted fairly, consistently and in accordance with relevant legislation
  • Selection will be based solely on merit and the ability to perform the job requirements
  • Equal opportunity will be given to all applicants regardless of race, gender, age, disability, religion, or any other protected characteristic
  • All vacancies will be advertised internally before external recruitment unless circumstances require otherwise
  • Recruitment processes will be transparent and documented

Recruitment Process

  1. Job Requisition: Department manager submits a staffing request with job description and justification
  2. Job Posting: HR posts the vacancy internally and/or externally as appropriate
  3. Application Screening: HR reviews applications against job requirements and shortlists candidates
  4. Interviews: Structured interviews conducted by hiring manager and HR representative
  5. Selection: Best candidate selected based on merit and fit with company culture
  6. Offer and Onboarding: Formal offer extended and onboarding process initiated

Required Documentation

  • Job Description
  • Person Specification
  • Application Form
  • Interview Assessment Forms
  • Reference Check Forms
  • Offer Letter Template

1.2 Induction Policy

Purpose

To ensure all new employees receive a comprehensive introduction to the company, their role, and the support needed to become effective team members.

Scope

This policy applies to all new employees, including permanent, temporary, and contract staff.

Objectives

  • Welcome new employees and make them feel valued
  • Provide essential information about the company, policies, and procedures
  • Clarify job responsibilities and performance expectations
  • Introduce key colleagues and establish working relationships
  • Ensure compliance with health, safety, and legal requirements
  • Set the foundation for ongoing development and success

Induction Program

Day 1

  • Welcome and introduction to team
  • Workstation setup and IT access
  • HR documentation and policies overview
  • Health and safety briefing
  • Office tour and facilities introduction

Week 1

  • Department introduction and role clarification
  • Meeting with key stakeholders
  • Training on essential systems and processes
  • Review of company policies and procedures
  • Setting initial objectives and expectations

Month 1

  • Regular check-ins with manager
  • Progress review and feedback
  • Additional training as required
  • Integration into team activities
  • Probation progress discussion

Responsibilities

Role Responsibility
HR Coordinate induction program, provide documentation, conduct HR-related sessions
Manager Prepare for arrival, provide job-specific training, set objectives, conduct regular check-ins
Buddy Provide informal support, answer questions, help with integration
New Employee Engage actively in induction, ask questions, complete required documentation

1.3 Employment Standards

Purpose

To establish clear standards for employee behavior and performance that support a positive, productive workplace.

Employee Expectations

  • Maintain professional conduct at all times
  • Treat colleagues, customers, and stakeholders with respect
  • Perform duties to the best of your ability
  • Follow company policies and procedures
  • Protect company assets and confidential information
  • Report concerns through appropriate channels
  • Support a positive and inclusive work environment

Professional Conduct

  • Arrive on time and be prepared for work
  • Communicate professionally in all interactions
  • Dress appropriately according to company dress code
  • Maintain confidentiality of company and client information
  • Avoid conflicts of interest
  • Use company resources responsibly
Non-Compliance: Failure to meet employment standards may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment.

1.4 Standards of Business Conduct

Purpose

To establish ethical standards and expectations for all business activities and interactions.

Principles

  • Act with integrity and honesty in all business dealings
  • Comply with all applicable laws and regulations
  • Avoid conflicts of interest and disclose potential conflicts
  • Protect company and client confidential information
  • Treat all stakeholders fairly and with respect
  • Report any unethical behavior or violations

Conflicts of Interest

A conflict of interest occurs when personal interests interfere or appear to interfere with company interests.

Examples include:

  • Financial interests in competitors or suppliers
  • Employment of relatives in positions of influence
  • Accepting gifts or entertainment that could influence decisions
  • Using company resources for personal gain
  • Outside employment that conflicts with company duties
Action Required: All potential conflicts must be disclosed to HR and management for review and resolution.

Gifts and Entertainment

  • Employees should not accept gifts, entertainment, or favors that could influence or appear to influence business decisions
  • Modest gifts of nominal value in the normal course of business are acceptable
  • Cash or cash equivalents, lavish entertainment, and gifts from parties seeking business decisions are prohibited

1.5 Internet and Email Usage Policy

Purpose

To establish guidelines for the appropriate use of company internet and email systems.

Scope

This policy applies to all employees using company-provided internet access and email systems.

Acceptable Use

  • Business-related research and communication
  • Professional development and learning
  • Limited personal use during breaks, provided it does not interfere with work
  • Communication with colleagues and external stakeholders

Prohibited Use

  • Accessing or distributing inappropriate, offensive, or illegal content
  • Downloading unauthorized software or files
  • Using personal email for business communications
  • Sharing confidential information externally
  • Engaging in activities that could harm company reputation
  • Excessive personal use that impacts productivity
  • Attempting to bypass security measures

Security Requirements

  • Do not share passwords or login credentials
  • Report suspicious emails or security concerns immediately
  • Lock computer when leaving workstation
  • Use strong passwords and change them regularly
Monitoring: The company reserves the right to monitor internet and email usage to ensure compliance with this policy and to protect company interests.

Section 2: Managing Employee Performance

2.1 Performance Appraisal Policy

Purpose

To establish a fair and consistent process for evaluating employee performance and supporting continuous improvement.

Objectives

  • Provide regular feedback on performance
  • Identify strengths and areas for development
  • Set clear objectives and expectations
  • Support career development and growth
  • Recognize and reward good performance
  • Address performance issues constructively

Appraisal Cycle

Review Type Frequency
Annual Review Formal annual performance review
Mid-Year Review Mid-year progress review
Ongoing Regular informal feedback and check-ins

Appraisal Process

  1. Self-Assessment: Employee completes self-assessment of performance against objectives
  2. Manager Assessment: Manager evaluates performance based on objectives and competencies
  3. Review Meeting: Manager and employee discuss performance, achievements, and development needs
  4. Objective Setting: New objectives set for the upcoming period
  5. Development Planning: Training and development needs identified and planned

Performance Ratings

Rating Description
Exceptional Consistently exceeds expectations in all areas
Strong Meets and often exceeds expectations
Competent Consistently meets expectations
Developing Meeting some but not all expectations
Unsatisfactory Not meeting expectations

Purpose

To establish clear procedures for managing employee absence and supporting attendance.

Types of Absence

Type Description
Sick Leave Absence due to illness or injury
Annual Leave Planned vacation time
Personal Leave Leave for personal matters
Emergency Leave Unplanned leave for emergencies
Maternity/Paternity Leave Leave for new parents

Notification Requirements

2.3 Disciplinary Procedure

Purpose

To establish a fair and consistent approach to dealing with misconduct and performance issues.

Principles

  • Disciplinary action will be applied fairly and consistently
  • Employees will be informed of allegations and given opportunity to respond
  • Employees may be accompanied at disciplinary meetings
  • Decisions will be based on evidence and thorough investigation
  • Right of appeal will be provided

Disciplinary Stages

Stage Action
Stage 1 Informal Discussion - Manager discusses concerns with employee and agrees actions
Stage 2 First Written Warning - Formal warning for continued or more serious issues
Stage 3 Final Written Warning - Serious warning indicating risk of dismissal
Stage 4 Dismissal - Termination of employment for continued or gross misconduct

Gross Misconduct

The following may be treated as gross misconduct warranting immediate dismissal:

  • Theft, fraud, or dishonesty
  • Violence or physical assault
  • Serious harassment or discrimination
  • Serious breach of health and safety
  • Gross negligence causing serious damage
  • Serious breach of confidentiality
  • Being under influence of drugs or alcohol at work

Appeals

Employees have the right to appeal any disciplinary decision within 5 working days.

2.4 Capability Procedure

Purpose

To provide a fair process for addressing situations where an employee is unable to perform their job to the required standard.

Note: Capability issues differ from misconduct - they relate to ability rather than behavior.

Possible Causes

  • Lack of skills or knowledge
  • Health issues affecting performance
  • Changes in job requirements
  • Personal circumstances affecting work

Capability Process

Stage Action
Stage 1 Initial Discussion - Identify the issue and discuss with employee
Stage 2 Support and Development - Provide training, coaching, or other support
Stage 3 Review Period - Set review period with clear targets
Stage 4 Further Review - Assess progress and determine next steps
Stage 5 Final Action - If no improvement, consider redeployment or dismissal

Support Measures

  • Additional training and development
  • Mentoring or coaching
  • Reasonable adjustments
  • Modified duties or hours
  • Occupational health assessment

Section 3: Employee Concerns

3.1 Grievance Procedure

Purpose

To provide employees with a fair and effective process for raising concerns about their employment.

Scope

Employees may raise grievances about any aspect of their employment including working conditions, relationships, or treatment.

Principles

  • All grievances will be treated seriously and confidentially
  • Employees will not suffer detriment for raising a grievance
  • Issues will be addressed promptly and fairly
  • Right to be accompanied at formal meetings

Grievance Stages

Stage Action
Stage 1 Informal Resolution - Try to resolve the issue informally with immediate manager
Stage 2 Formal Grievance - Submit written grievance to HR if informal resolution fails
Stage 3 Grievance Meeting - Meeting held to discuss grievance and investigate
Stage 4 Decision - Written decision provided within reasonable timeframe
Stage 5 Appeal - Right to appeal decision to senior management

Timeframes

Action Timeframe
Acknowledgement 3 working days
Meeting Within 10 working days of receipt
Decision Within 5 working days of meeting
Appeal 5 working days to submit appeal

3.2 Prevention of Bullying and Harassment

Purpose

To create a work environment free from bullying and harassment where all employees are treated with dignity and respect.

Definitions

Term Definition
Harassment Unwanted conduct that violates a person's dignity or creates an intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating, or offensive environment.
Bullying Offensive, intimidating, malicious, or insulting behavior that undermines, humiliates, or injures the recipient.
Sexual Harassment Unwanted conduct of a sexual nature that affects the dignity of the person.

Examples of Unacceptable Behavior

  • Verbal abuse, insults, or offensive jokes
  • Physical intimidation or threats
  • Unwanted physical contact
  • Spreading malicious rumors
  • Exclusion from activities or information
  • Unreasonable criticism or micromanagement
  • Offensive emails, messages, or images
Consequences: Harassment and bullying are treated as serious misconduct and may result in disciplinary action up to dismissal.

3.3 Whistleblowing Policy

Purpose

To enable employees to report concerns about wrongdoing without fear of reprisal.

Protected Disclosures

  • Criminal offenses
  • Failure to comply with legal obligations
  • Miscarriages of justice
  • Danger to health and safety
  • Environmental damage
  • Deliberate concealment of any of the above

Protection for Whistleblowers

  • No employee will suffer detriment for making a disclosure in good faith
  • Confidentiality will be maintained where possible
  • Investigation will be conducted fairly
  • Support will be provided throughout the process

Procedure

  1. Raise Concern: Report concern to immediate manager or HR
  2. Alternative Reporting: If concern involves manager, report to senior management or designated officer
  3. Investigation: Appropriate investigation conducted
  4. Outcome: Feedback provided on outcome where appropriate

4.2 Drug and Alcohol Policy

Purpose

To ensure a safe and healthy work environment by addressing the risks associated with drugs and alcohol.

Standards

  • Employees must not be under the influence of drugs or alcohol at work
  • Prescription medications must be declared if they may affect work
  • Illegal drugs are strictly prohibited on company premises
  • Alcohol consumption during work hours is prohibited except at authorized events

Support Available

  • Employees with substance issues are encouraged to seek help
  • Confidential support available through Employee Assistance Program
  • Time off for treatment may be granted
  • Return to work support provided
Consequences: Being under the influence at work or possessing illegal substances is gross misconduct and may result in dismissal.
